02 · Direction

Clean, modern, exact

The strategy wants two sentences in the reviews: “the app works in court” and “I got paid faster”. Clepso should feel like a precise instrument: light paper-like surfaces, a cool graphite neutral ramp, one clear cobalt accent, hairlines instead of shadows, tight radii, one typeface at three weights, and tabular numbers everywhere. No decoration carries meaning that a word or a number could carry better.

Principle 1

Built for the hallway

One-handed reach, 44pt targets, the primary action at the thumb, glanceable state through pills and dots, and a dark theme that doesn’t light up a courtroom. Nothing destructive is one tap.

Principle 2

Money and time are type

Amounts, hours and identifiers have their own styles: tabular figures, dimmed cents, mono numbers. Every amount has a status next to it. Every hour has a rate behind it.

Principle 3

Lists first, cards for the exception

Grouped lists with hairline separators carry the product. A card marks the one thing that isn’t a list item: the running timer, Review your day, a deadline, a trust warning.

Principle 4

Colour is a promise

Cobalt means “act” or “selected”. Green means paid or done. Amber means within a week. Red means late or at risk. Teal means court or system. Nothing else is coloured.

Principle 5

Plain language, disclosed AI

Client-facing copy names the next dated event. AI suggestions are grey, labelled, and applied only by hand. The disclosure line lives where the rule requires it.

Principle 6

One system, three surfaces

The same tokens drive mobile, web and the client app. Web adds density and multi-pane layout; the client app softens the voice. Nothing is redrawn per platform.

Colour Cobalt on graphite

One accent

Cobalt {{A:primary:light}} in light, lifted to {{A:primary:dark}} in dark so it passes on dark surfaces. It appears on one filled element per view, on selection, on links and on the current stage. Everything else is graphite.

Four reserved states

Success, warning, danger and a teal info hue. Teal was chosen so court dates and system notices never read as the blue accent. Status colours never decorate and never appear in charts.

Graphite neutrals

A cool ramp (hue ≈ 222°) that leans toward the cobalt without competing with it. Light canvas {{T:surface-canvas:light}}, ink {{T:ink-primary:light}}; dark canvas {{T:surface-canvas:dark}}, ink {{T:ink-primary:dark}}.

Typography two families, three weights

Geist · everything you read

High x-height, distinct numerals with real tabular figures, nine weights, excellent at 13–15px on both platforms. One family from the 11px overline to the 32px greeting; titles tighten to −0.02em so large text stays crisp. Ships as @expo-google-fonts/geist.

Geist Mono · records

Matter numbers, invoice numbers, UTBMS codes, account suffixes and the timer. It says “this is a record” without a label, and it keeps the timer from jittering.

Why no display face

The first draft used a serif for one line per screen. It read as editorial rather than modern, so it is gone: the greeting is now title-1 on mobile and display on web, in Geist. If a brand voice is wanted later it belongs in marketing and onboarding, not in the working screens.

03 · Navigation

Four places and one action

Mobile navigation is rebuilt around the strategy’s sequencing. Today is the daily loop (review captured time, agenda, who to nudge). Matters is triage and detail. Capture is the centre button on every screen. Calendar holds the docket and court dates. Billing is unbilled → invoices → payments → trust. Inbox (leads, client messages, approvals) starts as a header icon and takes the Calendar slot in H2 when the agenda folds into Today. Tasks and Time stop being destinations: tasks live in Today and inside matters; time lives in Capture and Billing.

Web mirrors the same model with a sidebar grouped into Work and Money, a ⌘K command palette, and the running timer in the top bar.
